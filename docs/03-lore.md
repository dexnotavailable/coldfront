# 03 · Story and lore

This is the lore bible. The owner asked for fleshed-out story and lore; everything here is a proposal the owner can veto. Items the owner hasn't confirmed yet are listed in `09-open-questions.md`.

**Rule for the agent:** lore is *found*, not told. No walls of text, no long cutscenes. See §9 for how the game delivers it, and §10 for what stays secret.

---

## 1. The premise (player-facing)

> Kaldmark was the heart of the Old Crown, until its last High King dug too deep. The land broke into rings of wrongness around the Nadir, and something now sits at the bottom of the world, wearing a crown.
>
> Every Thaw, the Stewards lead new kings in from beyond the Rim to push the Front down into the dark. Every Frost, the ice takes back what they built.
>
> This is the Coldfront: the war of steel against the deep.

"Coldfront" means two things in-world:
- The soldiers' word for the frontier, where cold steel meets the deep.
- The Frost's advancing edge, which ends every season.

The owner's core meaning, the coldness of steel, drives the tone.

---

## 2. Tone rules

- **Grounded.** People eat, freeze, get sick, get paid and get old. Institutions are practical. Everyone is human: no elves, dwarves or orcs. Monsters are things the deep made or unmade.
- **Magic is rare, costly and dangerous.** It has one source, the Wellspring, and one medium, mana. Channelling it without affinity burns you. Mages are precious.
- **Voice.** Terse, concrete, old-world. Short sentences. No modern slang, no winking, no purple prose.
- **Mystery.** Explain little, imply much. Every region has a story; the game shows pieces of it.

---

## 3. History

### The Basin (the deep past)
Kaldmark has always been a cold, rich basin, ringed by the ice of the Rim. Every few generations the **Frost** rolled inward from the Rim and buried the land. People learned to live between Frosts: stone halls, deep stores, hearth-fires kept burning through the dark. The oldest faith in Kaldmark is simply **the Hearth**, the fire you keep against the Frost.

### The Old Crown (about nine centuries ago)
The basin's peoples united under a crown seated at the Nadir, then a green highland above a clear lake called **the Mirror**. At the Nadir the first **ley wells** were found: springs of power that made the first mages. The Crown founded the first Academy, built roads and bridges across the basin, and grew rich.

### The Delving (six to three centuries ago)
The Crown's scholars learned that mana grew richer the deeper you went, so the Crown dug:
- the mines of Kurogane (now **the Old Workings**)
- the spiral **Delvers' Road**
- a deep capital called **Undervault** (now **the Buried City**)
- foundries near the bottom of the world (now **the Deep Forges**), with the **Deep Lift** between them

It was the arcane-industrial golden age: skystone lifts, moonsilver conduits, rails and relays. There was wealth beyond measure. But the deep changed the people who worked it. Mana-rot, **the Hollowing**, spread among the deep crews. The Hollowed forgot their names, then their faces, then themselves.

### The Wellspring (about three centuries ago)
The scholars warned of a Frost worse than any on record, one that would bury the Crown for good. **Kuon**, the last High King, led the Final Delving to the source of all mana: **the Wellspring**, a vast living light at the bottom of the world. He meant to take its power and end the Frost forever.

He bound himself to it.

### The Sundering (the day of the binding)
The Wellspring's power tore upward through the basin.
- The Nadir heaved up and blackened. The Mirror drained into the ring-sea now called **the Blackwater**.
- Around it, the land broke into the inner ring:
  - **Tasogare**, where the sky tore and the light drains away
  - **Ibara**, where the deep's heat broke through and its roots came up as thorns
  - **Hoshikuzu**, where the Wellspring's breath crystallises in the open
  - **the Sundered Isles**, where skystone tore the land loose and it fell upward

The Frost did not come that year. For a while, Kuon had won.

### The Titans' War
The **titans**, colossal and ancient beings of the Rim, older than the Crown, marched on the Nadir to end what Kuon had become. They died on the grey plain between the desert and the jungle, now **the Boneyard**. Their bones are still strong. Their marrow still holds power. Their dead still don't rest.

### The Wardens
Kuon, now **the King Below**, remade his court. Lords, servants and beasts of the old kingdom became the **Wardens**, each holding a Seat in the broken land and feeding him. While his court lives, he is still a king.

### The Turns
The Frost came back, stronger and far more often, bound now to the Wellspring's rhythm. Once every **Turn**, about twenty-six years by the basin's own calendar, it rolls in from the Rim and freezes the basin. When it melts, the land has shifted: the Wellspring remakes it. The Kaldfolk survive each Frost in the **Rimholds**, ice-halls deep in the Rim, and pour back into the basin with every Thaw.

After the Sundering, the **Stewards** appeared: quiet figures in grey who walk out of the Thaw and bring kings with them from beyond the Rim.

### Now
It's a new Turn. The ice has drawn back. The Stewards are walking in with a hundred new kings.

## 4. The Turn in-world (the season loop)

| Phase | Real time | In-world |
|---|---|---|
| **The Thaw** | season start | The ice withdraws. The land is new (new seed). Stewards lead kings in from the Rim; wanderers follow from the Rimholds. |
| **The Front** | weeks 1–25 (about 25 years on the basin's calendar) | Kings expand, fight and delve. |
| **The Frost** | week 26: the last 7 real days (the basin's last year, winter throughout) | The sky greys, snow thickens and the cold deepens across the whole world (the warning). On the last day the Frost takes the basin. The **Ledger of Kings** records the Turn. |

The Frost comes whether or not the King Below falls. A Turn in which he falls ends in a **clean Frost**: the sky over the Nadir clears for the first time, and the whole server sees it.

## 5. Cosmology

- **The Wellspring.** The source of mana: a vast light at the bottom of the world. It is alive in some sense, and it is hungry. Its breath rises through the rock as mana.
- **Mana.** The Wellspring's breath. It collects in three ways:
  - it crystallises where it gathers (**mana crystal**)
  - it pools where it rises (**ley wells**)
  - it runs as liquid at depth (**the Leyflow**)

  In Yomi it binds with what the dead leave behind into **soulglass**. Raw mana burns living things unless they have affinity or wards.
- **Moonsilver.** The only metal that carries mana cleanly. It forms where Tasogare's stolen light sinks into rock.
- **Skystone.** Stone the Wellspring pushes away. It "falls" upward. The Sundered Isles float on it; lifts are built with it.
- **Brimstone.** The deep's heat, crystallised. It burns hotter than any coal.
- **Titan bone and marrow.** The titans' remains. The bone is light and strong; the marrow holds old power.
- **Ichor.** The Wellspring's hunger, made liquid, in the Gut.
- **The Frost.** Kaldmark's ancient cycle, now bound to the Wellspring.
- **The Hollowing.** Mana-rot. People exposed to raw mana without affinity or wards slowly lose themselves. Many enemies are Hollowed: drowned pilgrims, lampless knights, deep crews.

## 6. The King Below

Kuon was a good king. He wanted to save his people from the Frost, and he gave the Wellspring everything to do it. What sits in the Pit now is the Wellspring's hunger wearing his crown.

- He rarely speaks. When he does, he speaks as a king: "my people", "my Crown", "my court".
- His Wardens are his court. **While any of his court lives, he is still a king.** In game terms, every living Warden adds to his regeneration.
- He is called only "the King Below" until a kingdom finds his name in the Buried City; each kingdom learns it for itself. **Kuon** (久遠) means "the long forever" in Court speech. He meant to end the Frost for good, and in a way he got his wish.

## 7. The Stewards

**What everyone sees.** A tall man in grey, hooded, carrying a small lantern. His face is never clear. One walks with every king.
- He speaks rarely, in short formal lines.
- He never lies, and he never explains what he *is*.
- **He is the king's first teacher.** At first moments, he points to the next step in one line: where to stack the stores, when to light the fires, what the cold will do.
- He never fights, and nothing ever attacks him. Enemies look straight through him.
- He works: hauls, tends, repairs, whatever suits his stats.
- When his king dies, he walks away toward the Rim. A new one walks in with the next king.
- He keeps an oath he states only once, on the first night: **"I keep the Crown until a king sits the throne."**

**Sample lines (the voice to match):**
- Arrival: "The ice has gone back. The land is yours to hold, if you can hold it."
- First stores (tutorial): "Food left in the open feeds the crows. Build a store."
- First night: "Keep the fires lit. The dark here is not empty."
- First winter: "The cold is patient. Be more patient."
- First rival sighted (tutorial): "Another crown walks these lands. Watch its riders."
- First descent: "Down is easy. Remember that."
- Deep (Layer 2+): "It is warm down here. It should not be."
- To the next king, after a king's death: "Another crown. Walk with me."
- If clicked with nothing to say: "I keep the Crown."

**The secret. Spoiler: never state it outright in-game.** The Stewards are what's left of Kuon's duty. At the binding, the part of the High King that wanted to protect his people tore away from the part that wanted the Wellspring. It walked back up the Descent as many grey keepers. That's why the deep never strikes them: it doesn't strike its own. They bring kings because what's left of Kuon wants to be ended.

The oath is deliberately ambiguous. Which throne? A king who reaches the Throne ends the King Below. The Ledger quietly notes that the King Below's face is not always the same from Turn to Turn.

**How it surfaces:**
1. The Steward's lines grow stranger the deeper his king delves.
2. Murals in the Buried City show the binding, and grey figures walking up out of the light.
3. The King Below's last words echo the Steward's phrasing.
4. When the King Below dies, every Steward in the world stops, kneels and stays still for one minute. The whole server sees it.

## 8. People and society

- **The Kaldfolk.** Humans of the basin: practical, cold-hardened, family-first. They are many peoples, bound together once by the Old Crown, and they speak three old tongues whose names mix freely today:
  - **Court speech** (Japanese-styled). The old capital's tongue, still spoken in the inner lands and the great houses. The Crown named its provinces, works and lords in it: Kurogane, Tasogare, Sekitei.
  - **Rim speech** (Northern European). The tongue of the Rim clans who ride out every Frost in the Rimholds. Plain, hard names: the Hearthlands, the Sallows, Brennvik.
  - **Scholars' Latin.** The Academy's language of mana, stars and craft: the Nadir, the Magister, the attuned's workings.
  - A few words came from traders beyond the Rim: the Selva, the title *Satrap*.
- **Wanderers.** Kaldfolk coming back from the Rimholds after the Frost. They keep trickling in all Turn, looking for a king who offers housing, food, safety and pay.
- **Neutral villages and camps.** Kaldfolk who resettled on their own. They can be won over or conquered.
- **Mages.** Called *the attuned*. Affinity is rare, perhaps one person in a few hundred, and training takes years. The official who runs mana and mages is the **Magister**.
- **Faith: the Hearth.** Households keep a hearth-fire; villages keep a hearth-shrine. The dead are burned, never buried, because buried dead in Kaldmark don't stay down. (This ties into the Boneyard and to recovering your fallen after battle.) The Hearth also kindles its own terrors: a person bound to a fire that must be fed, which Court speech calls *saiyaku*, a calamity (`13-units-classes-power.md` §15). Priests say it is the Hearth's answer to the Deep; the old say it is how the King Below began.
- **Money.** Gold **Crowns** and silver **Marks**, each struck with the minting king's stamp. Coins of fallen kings keep circulating.
- **Tier.** Peasant, craftsman and noble: in play, the three needs tiers (`05-systems.md` §7). A tier is earned by living standard, not birth.

## 9. How the game tells the story

1. **Discovery cards.** Entering a region for the first time shows its name and the first sentence of its story (§11), in engraved type. It fades after a few seconds.
2. **The Steward's lines** at milestones (§7). Never more than one line at a time.
3. **Descriptions.** Rare items, resources and blocks get one or two lines of flavour in their tooltip.
4. **Ruins and inscriptions.** Old Crown ruins in the Hearthlands hills, the Old Workings and the Buried City carry short inscriptions and murals.
5. **Wardens.** A title card when a Warden is engaged ("SHIMOTSUKI · THE HOARFATHER", then "Warden of Shirogane"; layout in `11-interface-catalogue.md` D9), and one line of last words when it dies.
6. **The Ledger.** An in-game codex that fills as you discover regions, Wardens, resources and inscriptions.
7. **The Chronicle.** An auto-written history of the server's season. Examples: "Week 3: Queen Aoi of Kiritani is the first to reach the Upper Deep." "Week 9: King Ivar of Brennvik and Livia of Solvara break Iwakura, the Mountain That Walks."
8. **The Frost.** The season-end event, and the Ledger of Kings.

## 11. Region stories (discovery cards, inscriptions, the Ledger)

The **first sentence** of each entry is the region's discovery-card line; the rest surfaces through inscriptions and the Ledger as they're found. A first sentence never gives away a secret from §10, and a later sentence that touches one (Tasogare's, for example) waits for that secret's reveal.

**Surface**
- **Hearthlands.** The Crown's breadbasket. Marshal Varn led the Crown's cavalry; when the Sundering came, he burned the granaries rather than surrender them, and he never stopped burning.
- **Shirogane.** The Frost lingers here longest. Shimotsuki, the Hoarfather, is an old giant of the Rim; his name means "frost month", and the cold seems to love him.
- **Kurogane.** The Crown's mines began here. Iwakura, "the rock where a god sits", is the mountain's heart: a stone titan that woke when the Delvers cut too deep.
- **Kogane.** The Crown's treasury province. Satrap Ozrem, the Crown's treasurer, sealed himself in his vault-tomb with the kingdom's gold, and the gold keeps him alive.
- **Boneyard.** Where the titans fell marching on the Nadir. The Gravewarden was their herald; now he keeps their graves and captains their dead.
- **Selva.** The Crown's pleasure gardens, gone wild. Vhessa was a garden-mage who fed the gardens with blood to keep them green after the Sundering.
- **Sallows.** Pilgrims walked the causeways to the shrines of the Mere. The Abbot led them into the fens when the water rose, and he kept preaching after they drowned.
- **Grey Mere.** Fishers call the Leviathan "the Mere's mother". It grew fat on the Crown's sunken fleet.
- **Tasogare.** The Sundering tore the sky here, and the light drains into the wound. Akari, the Lampless Queen, whose name means "light", put out her own lamps so she would not have to see what her king became.
- **Ibara.** Where the deep's heat broke through; the thorns are the underworld's roots pushing up. Gōka ("hellfire") is a drake of the deep that crowned itself with thorns.
- **Hoshikuzu.** Where the Wellspring's breath crystallises in the open. Hibiki ("echo"), the Resonant, was a choir of mages who tried to sing the storms still and were fused into one crystal.
- **Sundered Isles.** The land that fell upward. Admiral Kest commanded the Crown's river fleet; when the land tore loose, his harbour went with it, and he rules the islands as a corsair king.
- **Blackwater.** Once the Mirror, the lake of the Crown's capital. Now a black ring-sea.
- **Nadir.** The Crown's heart, heaved up and burned black. Severin, the Castellan, kept the Keep's gates for the High King, and he keeps them still.

**Layer 1**
- **Frost Hollows.** The Frost's cold seeps down and pools here. Tsurara ("icicle"), the Rime Wyrm, nests where it is coldest.
- **Old Workings.** The Crown's first deep mines. Foreman Gall ran the shifts. He still does.
- **Kagami Grottos.** Geodes of pure mana. Utsusemi, the Mirror Warden, shows you yourself and keeps what it shows. Its name is the shell a cicada leaves behind.
- **Ember Veins.** The deep's heat, running in veins. The Slagmother was born from the Crown's slag heaps.
- **Bone Pits.** Where titan bones sank through the earth. The Marrow Titan is a titan that never finished dying.
- **Root Halls.** The Selva's roots, reaching for the Wellspring. The Rootbound King was a Crown lord who hid among the roots and was taken by them.
- **Sporewood.** The Sallows' rot sinks here and blooms. The Bloom is one living thing the size of a region.
- **Drowned Caverns.** The Mere drains into these caves. The Blind Choir sings in the dark to see.

**Layer 2**
- **Sekitei.** The Crown's deep arboretum, turned to stone. The Gardener still tends it.
- **Buried City.** Undervault, the Crown's deep capital. The Regent rules it for a king who never came back up.
- **Leyflow.** The Wellspring's breath, running as a river. The Current is the river's will.
- **Great Shear.** Where the Undercrown broke away from the Maw. The Shear Matriarch's swarm nests in the cliff.
- **Hollow Sky.** A cavern so big it makes its own weather. Noctua, the Moth Sovereign, flies to every light and eats it.

**Layer 3 and the Pit**
- **The Gut.** The world's throat, where the Wellspring's hunger lives in the rock. The Maw That Feeds *is* the Gut.
- **Yomi.** The dead of every Frost wash down here as ash. Yomotsu, Queen of Ashes, rules the tides. Court speech named this place for the land of the dead.
- **Deep Forges.** The Crown's deepest foundries, now the King Below's armouries. Vorgrim was the Crown's master smith.
- **The Throne.** The Wellspring, and the one who sits before it.

## 12. Naming conventions

The owner's direction: names are **diverse**, with a strong Japanese/anime-styled strand alongside the European fantasy names. Every name comes from one of the three tongues (§8), and lists mix them.

- **Mix tongues everywhere.** A list of settlements, a roster of people or a Warden list is never all one tongue. Rough share for generated names: Court 45%, Rim 40%, Scholars' 15%, shifted by place: more Rim near the Rim and in the north-west, more Court in the inner lands and the three metal provinces (Shirogane, Kurogane, Kogane). Borrowed names are placed by hand, never generated.
- **Court speech** (Japanese-styled) is built from **real words with a fitting meaning**, never pseudo-Japanese syllable soup. If it isn't a real word or a well-formed compound, don't use it.
  - Place roots: kiri (mist), kuro (black), shiro (white), aka (red), taka (high), haya (swift), tsuki (moon), hoshi (star), kaze (wind), iwa or ishi (stone), yama (mountain), tani (valley), mori (forest), hara (field), kawa or -gawa (river), saka or -zaka (slope), mura (village), -jō (castle, for keeps).
  - Place examples: Kiritani ("mist valley"), Hayakawa ("swift river"), Kurosaka ("black slope"), Takamori ("high forest").
  - People: real Japanese given names and family names. Examples: Aoi Kiritani, Ren Takamori, Hana Kurosaka, Sora Hayakawa.
- **Rim speech** (Northern European compounds).
  - Place roots: Kald-, Sten-, Isen-, Grey-, Ash-, Brenn-, Hoar-, Frost-, Holm-, Ask-. Endings: -ford, -holm, -vik, -dal, -mark, -stead, -gard, -by, -watch. Examples: Brennvik, Askdal, Kaldby, Stenholm, Greyford, Eastwatch.
  - People: a Norse, Germanic and Slavic feel. Examples: Ivar Stenholt, Maren Askdal, Radek Holmgaard, Edda Brennvik.
- **Scholars' Latin** (academies, observatories, mage works, some towns).
  - Place roots: Sol-, Luc-, Aur-, Val-, Stell-, Nox-, Ver-. Endings: -ia, -ium, -ara, -ona, -ensis. Examples: Solvara, Aurentia, Valmora, Lucida.
  - People: Latin and Romance names. Examples: Livia Solvara, Cassian Aurel, Marisol Valmora.
- **Borrowed.** A few hand-placed names from beyond the Rim (Ozrem, Vhessa, the Selva, the title *Satrap*). Name generators never produce them.
- **People** have a given name and a family name, usually from one tongue per family; mixed families mix.
- **Never** use famous real people, famous fictional characters, joke names, or famous real places (Tokyo, Kyoto, Oslo and the like).
- **Enemies.** Plain English descriptive names: ash-fiend, the risen, mine-construct. No syllable soup.
- **Wardens.** A personal name plus a title ("Shimotsuki, the Hoarfather"; "Noctua, the Moth Sovereign"), or a title alone ("the Gravewarden"). The roster mixes tongues.
- **Kingdoms.** Players name their own; the default is "<Capital> Crown", e.g. "Kiritani Crown" or "Brennvik Crown".
- **Stable IDs.** Code refers to regions and layers only by their IDs (`02-world.md` §2, "Names and IDs"), never by display names, so renaming is a data change.
